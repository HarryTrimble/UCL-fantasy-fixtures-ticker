What the projections are, and are not

These are a third party's projections, and they are internally consistent: every team's projected goals for equals its opponent's projected goals against across all 288 team‑matches. What they do not carry is an uncertainty band, so two fixtures with the same projection are not distinguishable here. The market‑anchored solver on the model tab remains the route to that, and to a benchmark these can be scored against.

01

Define two targets, not one

λ against
Expected goals conceded. Drives defensive difficulty and clean‑sheet probability.
λ for
Expected goals scored. Drives attacking difficulty.

A fixture can be easy for your forwards and hard for your defenders. A single number cannot say so, which makes the split the model's most important structural choice. For player‑level returns, add a third layer: minutes probability × share of team output.

02

The core equation

log λfor   =  μ + ATTteam − DEFopp + h·home + Σ βi·ctxi
log λagst =  μ + ATTopp − DEFteam − h·home + Σ γi·ctxi

μ is the competition baseline goal rate. Fit β and γ on historical Champions League matches by maximum likelihood, with a low‑score correlation correction. No hand‑picked percentage weights anywhere in the model.

03

Team ratings

Blend by inverse‑variance weighting, not fixed shares. The market gets more weight as kickoff approaches; coefficient and Elo carry the early load.

Source Treatment Role
Market‑implied strength De‑vigged 1X2 and Asian handicap lines, converted to a goal‑expectation prior Sharpest single input
Club Elo Cross‑league comparable; no adjustment needed Stable backbone
Domestic xG for / against Opponent‑adjusted, scaled by league strength, exponential decay (half‑life ≈10 matches) Recent form signal
UEFA coefficient / pot Shrinkage prior; weight decays as match data accumulates Early‑season anchor
Squad availability Suspensions and registration omissions applied as a rating delta, not a separate term Match‑level correction
04

Context terms

Fitted, small, additive in log space. These are the genuinely competition‑specific terms. Expected magnitudes are sanity checks on the fit, not inputs.

Term Encoding Expected size
Venue Binary home indicator ≈0.30–0.35 goals total
Rest and congestion Days since last match, days to next domestic match — the sandwich effect lives here Small; raises rotation risk more than λ
Travel Great‑circle distance + time‑zone delta, one term Marginal
Stakes Matchday index, plus the qualification‑probability swing the result creates for each side. Replaces vague “motivation” Material at MD6–8
Two‑leg state Leg number and aggregate score state Large in settled second legs

Style variables — pressing intensity, set‑piece xG share, possession differential — shift variance more than expectation. Model them on the dispersion parameter, not on λ.

05

From λ to a rating

Rank λagainst across all league‑phase fixtures, take the percentile, map to buckets. Repeat separately for λfor. The bucket is for reading at a glance; the underlying number and its interval are what you act on.

Easiest decile Hardest decile
Always ship the interval

Two fixtures with the same mean difficulty and different predictive spread are not the same fixture. For one‑off differentials the spread is the entire decision.

07

Validation

Score with log‑loss and Brier on match outcomes and clean sheets, out of sample, against the market closing line and an Elo‑only model. Check calibration by decile, not just aggregate error.

If a term does not beat the market benchmark on its own increment, it is not earning its place. Cut it.

08

Market data → λ

Odds don't give you goals. Two steps get you there: strip the bookmaker's margin to recover true probabilities, then solve for the λ pair whose Poisson score distribution reproduces them. The totals line pins the sum; the 1X2 pins the split.

Step 1 — De‑vig
Invert each decimal price, then rescale so the book sums to 1. Power method (p = rawk) rather than proportional — it treats longshots better, which matters in a competition containing both Bayern and Sabah.
Step 2 — Solve the total
Find Λ = λh + λa such that a Poisson with mean Λ reproduces the de‑vigged over probability at the quoted line.
Step 3 — Solve the split
Hold Λ fixed and bisect the home share until independent Poisson marginals reproduce the de‑vigged home‑win probability. An Asian handicap line, where quoted, pins the same split more precisely.

Example prices, not a live feed

Match markets for the 2026/27 league phase are not yet published — only outright tournament prices. The prices above are illustrative. The solver is live: drop closing odds into data/ucl‑odds‑md1.json and lib/market‑lambda.js returns λ for every fixture in the file.

Where the market leads
The closing line is the sharpest public forecast of a football match. It already prices squad news, motivation, rest and crowd — every factor a hand‑weighted model tries to add by hand, and prices them better.
Where it can't help
Prices only exist near kickoff, so matchday‑6 planning in September still needs Elo and coefficient. Liquidity thins on the small fixtures, exactly where help is most needed. And a market‑only model can never beat the market — the edge lives in the Elo and xG terms.
Deliberately excluded
Hand‑set percentage weightsFalse precision. Either fit them or state them as additive deltas — never both.
Hostile‑atmosphere penaltiesSubjective, and already priced into Elo and the market line.
Altitude as its own termAn edge case in this competition; folded into travel if kept at all.
Any single‑number ratingWithout the attack/defence split it cannot answer the question people ask of it.
